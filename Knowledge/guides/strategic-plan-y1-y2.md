# RimWorld Strategic Plan — Years 1 and 2

Distilled from community guides (RimWorld Wiki, beginner guides, wealth-management material). Assumes vanilla, Crashlanded, temperate forest, Cassandra Strive-to-Survive. Where assumptions matter, they are called out so the agent can branch.

A RimWorld year is 4 quadrums of 15 days each. This guide covers the first 8 quadrums.

---

## Year 1 — Survival

The whole year is one coherent goal: **don't die, build the smallest viable engine.** Aggressive growth attracts raids the colony cannot survive. Wealth is the throttle.

### Y1 Q1 Spring (days 1–15) — Plant or starve

**Posture:** pure survival; wood is fine; wealth must stay low.

- **Food:** rice in rich soil ASAP; two zones, staggered planting; opportunistic small-game hunting.
- **Shelter:** single wooden barracks ~9×9, three beds; a research bench; a stockpile zone.
- **Mood:** wooden horseshoe pin or chess table for joy; mood debt is the silent killer.
- **Defense:** pistols / short bows only; no fortification; flee to chokepoints if threatened.
- **Labor bias:** Plants and Construction high; Mining low; Cooking by best-skilled colonist (raw food causes food poisoning).
- **Research priority:** Battery → Solar Panels → Air Conditioning (Crashlanded). Tribal start branches: Complex Clothing → Smithing.

**Q1 done-when:** all colonists sleeping under a roof, one solar+battery online, 30-day food stockpile or a clear path to one within 15 days.

### Y1 Q2 Summer (days 16–30) — Permanence

**Posture:** convert temporary structures to permanent; stone everything that can burn; keep wealth-per-day flat.

- **Construction:** Stonecutting unlocked → stone walls; replace wood roof over kitchen and freezer first; small private bedrooms (~4×4) for mood headroom; insulated freezer with cooler chain.
- **Food:** add corn for storage stability; haygrass if pack animals exist; freezer must be online before fall harvest.
- **Mood:** real rec room (table + horseshoe + chess); small dining room.
- **Defense:** bolt-action rifles or revolvers; first sandbag line at chokepoint; no turrets yet (components scarce).
- **Crafting:** apparel crafting if any colonist has Crafting passion (cheap mood from clothes).
- **Research priority:** Stonecutting → Smithing → Machining (queued) → Microelectronics (queued).

### Y1 Q3 Fall (days 31–45) — Stockpile and harden

**Posture:** winter is 15 days out; food and warmth are the only things that matter; defense gets hardened because raid points are catching up.

- **Food:** final harvest push; butcher hunted herds; smokeleaf for trade; pack the freezer (surplus is fine here, doesn't move wealth much).
- **Construction:** killbox shape complete — stone walls, single-tile entry, sandbags inside, dead-end approach; first IEDs / mini-turret tier if Machining is done.
- **Defense:** drill the chokepoint; assign roles; stockpile medicine.
- **Mood/Apparel:** parkas, tuques, heaters in bedrooms — hypothermia kills more first colonies than raids.
- **Labor:** all hands on harvest during the harvest window; defense drills outside it; component runs (mining steel, deconstructing junk).
- **Research priority:** Machining → Gunsmithing → Electric smelter (component recycling).

### Y1 Q4 Winter (days 46–60) — Inside work

**Posture:** survive; push research; do not expand wealth; build mood reserves for spring.

- **Food:** hydroponics ONLY if power surplus and components are available — usually skip year 1; hunting if biome supports.
- **Construction:** hospital corner (sterile-tile-able later); prison cell (recruitment is wealth-cheap and high-value); lavish single rooms only for break-risk colonists; beautify the dining/rec room (single biggest mood lever).
- **Mood:** sculpting for beauty + recreation simultaneously; watch breakdown risks closely.
- **Defense:** weapon tier transition — chain shotguns are the wiki's recommended threshold past which wealth concerns relax; first turret line behind chokepoint.
- **Labor:** heavy crafting + research; minimal outdoor work; recruit prisoners from fall raids.
- **Research priority:** Microelectronics → Hydroponics (queued) → Multi-analyzer → tier-2 weapons → Geothermal (if vent exists).

**Year 1 done-when:** ≥4 colonists alive, stone base, freezer with 60+ days food, killbox functional, bolt-action or better on every shooter, multi-analyzer or close to it.

## Year 2 — Engine

The colony stops reacting and starts compounding. Wealth-per-day can rise modestly because defense is real. The dominant risk shifts from environmental to **raid-point inflation** — wealth and colony age both push raid sizes up, and naive colonies fall behind here.

### Y2 Q1 Spring (days 61–75) — Specialize

**Posture:** convert generalists to specialists; recruit toward gaps; wealth growth is permitted but tracked.

- **Medical:** real hospital — sterile tile, vitals monitor, dedicated doctor work priority; prosthetics research starts to matter.
- **Food:** start a devilstrand patch (year-long grow, premium apparel material, strong mood-via-quality); expand rice/corn for population growth.
- **Trade crops:** drug crops (smokeleaf, psychoid) for trade or controlled mood.
- **Construction:** workshop room; bigger freezer; second power source — geothermal if vent available, otherwise watermill or wind+battery.
- **Defense:** begin replacing chain shotguns with assault rifles where shooter skill supports it; first mortar pad; EMP/incendiary shells reserved for mech clusters; trap corridor extension.
- **Labor:** lock in roles — 1 dedicated researcher, 1 cook, 1 crafter, 2 shooters; stop generalists doing everything.
- **Research priority:** Fabrication path (advanced components are the bottleneck for everything past this point); Hospital bed.

### Y2 Q2 Summer (days 76–90) — Diplomacy on

**Posture:** open the trade economy; goodwill is wealth-free power.

- **Trade:** first caravan to nearest friendly faction; gifts to lift goodwill above 75 for ally call-in; sell drugs, fine meals, simple-quality apparel; buy components, advanced components, plasteel.
- **Construction:** comms console if not already up; trade beacon stockpile; solar-flare backup (vanometric / standby batteries).
- **Defense:** anti-mech doctrine — doomsday or triple rocket launchers in reserve, EMP grenades for centipedes; plan for the first proper mech cluster (~day 120 in Cassandra).
- **Food:** convert food surplus to trade goods (pemmican for caravans, kibble for animals); animal training (dog school) for haulers.
- **Labor:** designate caravan-capable pawn; back-fill labor while away; expect real pawn-hour loss during caravans.
- **Research priority:** Multi-analyzer-gated techs → Gun turrets (improved) → Packaged survival meal.

### Y2 Q3 Fall (days 91–105) — The wealth wall

**Posture:** raid points are now meaningful; either harden further or trim wealth; no middle ground.

This is where most colonies die — not from a single event but from accumulating wealth without proportional defense. Compare wealth-velocity against defense-tier.

- **Defense tier check.** If not at all of: stone walls + chain shotguns or better on every shooter + functional killbox + 2+ turrets + mortars → halt expansion until parity is reached. Sappers and breachers begin appearing in raids; verify wall coverage.
- **Trade / wealth sink:** sink wealth into goodwill, research, and consumed items (drugs, meals, fuel) — none of these increase colony wealth. Sell off junk apparel and low-quality items aggressively.
- **Construction:** double-wall the kill corridor; embrasures if biome supports; power redundancy — no single failure can down the freezer.
- **Food:** devilstrand harvest (year+ grow) lands here; maintain freezer integrity through fall events.
- **Crafting:** re-quality apparel and weapons; dump low-quality variants.
- **Research priority:** Advanced fabrication, gun turrets, sterile materials, charged shot.

### Y2 Q4 Winter (days 106–120) — Choose the endgame

**Posture:** pick a victory condition; allocate research and wealth toward it; stop hedging.

By the end of year 2 the colony should be on one of these trajectories:

- **Ship launch** — start spaceship hull research; acquire AI persona core (raid drop or quest).
- **Royal favor** (Royalty DLC) — accept quests, build throne room, gain titles.
- **Archonexus** — start the wealth-collection arc (different posture; allows wealth accumulation).
- **Maintenance / sandbox** — accept indefinite play; keep raid floor stable.

The Mayor's `colony_objective` should be set explicitly here so ministers' goal rankings align.

**Year 2 done-when:** ≥6 colonists, advanced components in supply, full defense tier, ≥75 goodwill with at least one faction, multi-analyzer + a fabrication-tier facility, a chosen endgame trajectory.

## Cross-cutting heuristics

### Wealth velocity
Raid points scale with wealth and colony age. Watching *velocity* (wealth gained per quadrum) matters more than absolute wealth. Defense-tier should track velocity, not absolute wealth.

### Wealth-free power
Three sinks raise effective colony power without raising raid points: research progress, faction goodwill, and consumed goods (food, drugs, fuel, medicine). Prefer these over stockpiled items, decorated furniture, or excess apparel.

### The chain shotgun threshold
Wiki community consensus: once every shooter has a chain shotgun (or better), stone walls, and a functional killbox, wealth concerns relax sharply. This is the load-bearing milestone — it is the difference between reactive and proactive play.

### Mood as currency
Mood is a continuous resource, not a binary. Beauty in dining + rec + bedroom rooms compounds. A break-risk colonist costs more pawn-hours than a single luxury bedroom. Sculptures are wealth-cheap mood relative to other levers.

### Recruit > breed (early)
Recruiting prisoners from raids is wealth-cheap (the prisoner is wealth either way) and skill-flexible (you pick whom). Pregnancies arrive late and cost mood + medical capacity. Recruit through year 1; breed in year 2+.

### Caravan opportunity cost
Sending a caravan removes a pawn for many days. Real cost is the pawn-hours lost on base, not the silver spent. Only caravan when the alternative (waiting for a trader) is materially worse.

### Storyteller awareness
Cassandra escalates linearly with time and wealth. Phoebe is more relaxed but the same logic applies on a slower curve. Randy ignores the curve — preparedness must be constant rather than scheduled.
